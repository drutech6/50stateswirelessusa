  The JBL Tune 500 Wired On-Ear Headphones are a battery-free, plug-and-play audio solution focused heavily on deep low-end output. Budget reviewers highlight their reliable, latency-free wired connection and lightweight frame, making them a highly practical choice for straightforward classroom lessons, office voice calls, and legacy devices with a headphone jack. 
  Core Technical Specifications & Design
  
      32mm Dynamic Drivers: Imbued with signature JBL Pure Bass tuning to prioritize thick, punchy bass lines and rich lower-frequency tracks.
      Lag-Free 3.5mm Jack: Uses a fixed, permanently attached 46-inch audio cable. It draws power entirely from the source device, requiring zero recharging or batteries.
      Tangle-Free Flat Cable: The audio cord is designed with a specialized flat ribbon profile to prevent knots and tangles during storage.
      1-Button In-Line Remote: A single mechanical click-button built into the wire handles call management, media playback pausing, track skipping, and smartphone assistant activation.
      Foldable On-Ear Chassis: Weighs just 148 grams. The earcups swivel and fold inward toward the padded headband to easily slip into a backpack. 
  
  Transitioning from the Tune 710BT
  to the Tune 500
  If you are considering these alongside the over-ear JBL Tune 710BT
  you just looked at, the hardware changes are distinct:
  
      Form Factor Difference: The Tune 710BT
      is an over-ear headset that surrounds the outer ear for better isolation. The Tune 500
      is a smaller on-ear design that rests directly on the ear cartilage.
      Connectivity Limits: The Tune 710BT
      lets you roam cord-free via Bluetooth. The Tune 500
      requires a physical connection, meaning modern smartphones will require a USB-C or Lightning to 3.5mm dongle adapter.
      On-Board Controls: While the 710BT
      has a full set of volume adjustments on the earcup rim, the Tune 500
      relies completely on its single-button wire or your host device to scale volume up or down.
